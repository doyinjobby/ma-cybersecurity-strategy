# M&A Cybersecurity Strategy

**Author:** Dr. Olajumoke Bello, PhD  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Classification:** Public Portfolio Sample  
**Status:** Illustrative M&A Cybersecurity Strategy



## 1. Executive Summary

This M&A Cybersecurity Strategy provides a structured, risk-based approach for conducting cybersecurity due diligence, integrating information security capabilities and maintaining business resilience throughout the merger and acquisition lifecycle.

Mergers and acquisitions can create opportunities for organisational growth, market expansion and operational efficiency. However, they can also introduce cybersecurity, privacy, regulatory, operational and third-party risks that may affect transaction value, delay integration and increase exposure to cyber incidents.

The strategy therefore embeds cybersecurity throughout the transaction lifecycle, from pre-acquisition due diligence through integration and post-acquisition assurance.

The objective is not simply to identify technical vulnerabilities. Cybersecurity should support informed business decisions, protect transaction value and enable the secure integration of people, processes, technology, data and third parties.

The approach is aligned with recognised cybersecurity, risk and resilience frameworks including:

- ISO/IEC 27001:2022
- ISO/IEC 27002:2022
- ISO 27005
- ISO 22301
- NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0
- SOC 2 Trust Services Criteria
- UK GDPR and Data Protection Act 2018
- CIS Critical Security Controls v8

## 2. Strategic Objectives

The strategy focuses on five strategic objectives.

### 2.1 Cybersecurity Due Diligence

Conduct comprehensive cybersecurity due diligence to identify technical, operational, regulatory and third-party risks that may materially affect the acquisition.

### 2.2 Secure Integration

Develop a structured security integration roadmap that progressively aligns the acquired organisation with the acquiring organisation's Information Security Management System (ISMS), governance model and security standards.

### 2.3 Operational Resilience

Strengthen Business Continuity Management, Disaster Recovery and incident response capabilities to minimise disruption during and following integration.

### 2.4 Third-Party and Supplier Risk

Assess suppliers and outsourced service providers to establish whether contractual, regulatory, cybersecurity and resilience requirements continue to be satisfied throughout the transition.

### 2.5 Continuous Governance and Assurance

Establish security metrics, executive reporting, risk management, compliance monitoring and ongoing assurance to support long-term business objectives.

## 3. M&A Cybersecurity Lifecycle

The cybersecurity strategy follows four principal stages.

### Stage 1 — Pre-Deal: Cybersecurity Due Diligence

The objective is to identify material cybersecurity liabilities before completion of the transaction.

Key activities include:

- Review ISO 27001, SOC 2 and other relevant assurance evidence
- Assess cybersecurity governance and risk management
- Review historical security incidents and data breaches
- Assess identity and privileged access controls
- Review vulnerability management
- Assess cloud and technology security
- Review critical suppliers and third-party dependencies
- Assess business continuity and disaster recovery
- Identify risks capable of affecting transaction value

Cybersecurity findings are recorded and prioritised to support informed transaction decisions.

---

### Stage 2 — Pre-Closing: Security Integration Planning

This phase prepares the security approach for combining two organisational and technology environments.

Key activities include:

- Define Identity and Access Management integration requirements
- Identify privileged access risks
- Map security policies and control gaps
- Establish Day-1 security priorities
- Identify risks arising from network interconnection
- Determine security monitoring requirements
- Define data migration security requirements
- Establish risk ownership and remediation priorities
- Develop the post-acquisition integration roadmap



### Stage 3 — Post-Closing: Security Integration

Following completion, priority security controls are implemented as the organisations begin integration.

Activities may include:

- Maintain network segmentation until environments have been appropriately assessed
- Deploy required endpoint security controls
- Integrate identity and access management
- Review and restrict privileged access
- Secure and monitor data migration
- Integrate security logging and monitoring
- Remediate critical vulnerabilities
- Align incident response processes
- Deliver security awareness for acquired personnel
- Address priority third-party risks

---

### Stage 4 — Post-Integration and Optimisation

The final phase transitions the combined organisation toward sustainable security governance and continuous improvement.

Activities include:

- Establish unified security monitoring
- Complete ISMS and policy integration
- Retire redundant or legacy systems securely
- Conduct security testing and assurance
- Review residual risks
- Complete management review
- Establish executive cybersecurity reporting
- Capture lessons learned
- Transition outstanding activities into continuous improvement

## 4. Cybersecurity Governance

Effective M&A cybersecurity requires cross-functional governance rather than security operating independently.

### Key Stakeholders and Responsibilities

- **Executive Sponsor** — Provides sponsorship and approves material M&A risks
- **CIO** — Leads technology integration
- **CISO** — Provides cybersecurity leadership and risk oversight
- **Information Security** — Conducts security assessments and coordinates security controls
- **Enterprise Architecture** — Supports secure integration of systems, applications and networks
- **Legal** — Reviews security-related contractual and regulatory requirements
- **Privacy** — Oversees personal data and privacy requirements
- **Human Resources** — Supports identity lifecycle, workforce transition and awareness
- **Procurement** — Coordinates supplier contracts and third-party risk
- **IT Operations** — Supports infrastructure integration and operational stability
- **Business Continuity** — Coordinates resilience and recovery requirements
- **PMO** — Tracks integration milestones, dependencies, risks and actions

Material cybersecurity risks should have clear ownership, treatment actions, target dates and executive oversight.

## 5. Cybersecurity Due Diligence Framework

The due diligence assessment should consider the following domains:
### Due Diligence Assessment Domains

- **Governance** — ISMS, policies and security governance — *Output: Gap Assessment*
- **Risk Management** — Cybersecurity risk register and risk treatment — *Output: Risk Report*
- **Identity & Access** — IAM, MFA and privileged access — *Output: Access Findings*
- **Vulnerability Management** — Vulnerabilities and remediation practices — *Output: Technical Findings*
- **Cloud Security** — AWS, Azure, M365 and other cloud services — *Output: Cloud Assessment*
- **Third Parties** — Supplier assurance and dependencies — *Output: Vendor Risk Report*
- **Business Continuity** — BCP, DR and BIA — *Output: Resilience Assessment*
- **Data Protection** — Personal data, privacy and data flows — *Output: Privacy Findings*
- **Incident Management** — Incident history and response capability — *Output: Incident Readiness Findings*

Detailed findings should feed into the M&A Cybersecurity Risk Register and influence transaction and integration priorities.



## 6. Risk and Deal-Impact Approach

Cybersecurity findings should be assessed according to their likelihood, impact and potential effect on the transaction.

Findings may be classified as:

- **Deal Breaker** — risk may make the acquisition unacceptable
- **Pre-Close Condition** — remediation or contractual protection required before completion
- **Day-1 Priority** — immediate action required following acquisition
- **30–90 Day Remediation** — managed through the integration programme
- **Longer-Term Improvement** — incorporated into continuous security improvement

This enables cybersecurity risk to be considered in the context of transaction value, regulatory exposure, operational resilience and integration complexity.



## 7. Day-1 Security Priorities

Immediate post-acquisition priorities should focus on stabilising the security environment and reducing exposure.

Priority areas include:

- Privileged account review
- MFA validation
- Identity and access review
- Network segmentation
- Remote access and VPN review
- Endpoint detection and response coverage
- Backup and recovery validation
- Central security logging
- SIEM integration
- Initial threat hunting
- Critical vulnerability remediation
- Incident escalation arrangements

Day-1 activities should be risk-driven and should avoid unnecessary disruption to critical business services.

---

## 8. Post-Acquisition Security Integration

The integration programme transitions due-diligence findings into structured remediation and security improvement.

The approach is organised across:

### Days 0–30 — Assess, Stabilise and Prioritise

Establish asset visibility, validate access, assess suppliers, identify critical vulnerabilities and confirm immediate security risks.

### Days 31–60 — Align, Remediate and Integrate

Harmonise policies, integrate IAM, remediate critical vulnerabilities, review continuity arrangements and strengthen security awareness.

### Days 61–90 — Assure, Govern and Optimise

Integrate the ISMS, conduct internal assurance and penetration testing, establish executive reporting and complete management review.

The detailed roadmap is maintained as a separate portfolio deliverable.

---

## 9. Security Measurement and Assurance

Security integration should be supported by measurable indicators.

Example measures include:

- Percentage of critical assets identified
- Percentage of privileged accounts reviewed
- Percentage of critical suppliers assessed
- Critical vulnerability remediation performance
- IAM integration progress
- Security awareness completion
- BCP/DR review completion
- ISMS control integration progress
- Internal audit findings
- Penetration testing remediation
- Residual high-risk findings
- Executive reporting completion

Metrics should demonstrate both remediation progress and the effectiveness of the combined security environment.

---

## 10. Governance and Reporting Cadence

A structured reporting cadence supports accountability throughout the integration programme.

### Reporting Activities

- **M&A Steering Committee** — Weekly — Executive Sponsor, CIO, CISO and PMO
- **Cyber Risk Review** — Weekly — Information Security and Enterprise Architecture
- **Third-Party Risk Review** — Bi-weekly — Procurement, Vendor Risk and Legal
- **Integration Status Report** — Weekly — PMO and Executive Sponsor
- **Executive Cybersecurity Dashboard** — Monthly — Executive Leadership
- **Management Review** — Monthly — CISO, CIO and Executive Sponsor

Reporting frequency should be adjusted according to transaction complexity and risk exposure.

---

## 11. Target Security State

The intended outcome is a combined organisation in which:

- Material cybersecurity risks are understood and owned
- Critical assets are identified
- Privileged access is appropriately governed
- Security monitoring covers critical environments
- Critical vulnerabilities are managed within agreed risk tolerances
- Third-party risks are subject to appropriate assurance
- Business continuity and disaster recovery arrangements are validated
- Security policies and governance are aligned
- The acquired environment is integrated into the ISMS
- Executive leadership receives meaningful cybersecurity assurance
- Outstanding risks are transitioned into continuous improvement

---

## 12. Strategic Outcome

Successful M&A cybersecurity depends on integrating governance, risk management, security and resilience from the earliest stages of the transaction.

The strategy establishes the following lifecycle:

**Due Diligence → Risk Identification → Deal Decision → Integration Planning → Day-1 Security → 30-60-90 Day Integration → Assurance → Continuous Improvement**

This approach enables cybersecurity to protect transaction value while supporting secure and resilient business integration.
