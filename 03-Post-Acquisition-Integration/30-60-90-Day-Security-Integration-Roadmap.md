# 30-60-90 Day Post-Acquisition Security Integration Roadmap

**Author:** Dr. Olajumoke Bello, PhD  
**Repository:** M&A Cybersecurity Strategy  
**Document Version:** 1.0  
**Classification:** Portfolio Sample  
**Status:** Illustrative Post-Acquisition Security Integration Deliverable

---

## 1. Purpose

This roadmap provides a structured 90-day approach for integrating cybersecurity capabilities following a merger or acquisition.

The roadmap translates cybersecurity due-diligence findings into prioritised post-acquisition actions designed to reduce immediate security exposure, protect critical business services, align governance and controls, strengthen operational resilience and establish sustainable security oversight across the combined organisation.

The approach is divided into three phases:

- **Days 0–30:** Assess, Stabilise and Prioritise
- **Days 31–60:** Align, Remediate and Integrate
- **Days 61–90:** Assure, Govern and Optimise

---

## 2. Strategic Objectives

The first 90 days of post-acquisition cybersecurity integration should:

- Establish visibility of critical assets, systems, data and dependencies
- Validate privileged and high-risk access
- Identify and prioritise material cybersecurity risks
- Assess critical third-party and supplier dependencies
- Address critical and high-risk vulnerabilities
- Align security policies and governance arrangements
- Integrate identity and access management capabilities
- Strengthen business continuity and disaster recovery arrangements
- Progress integration into the Information Security Management System (ISMS)
- Establish measurable security assurance and executive reporting

---

# Phase 1: Days 0–30 — Assess, Stabilise and Prioritise

## 3. Phase Objective

The first 30 days focus on establishing security visibility and reducing immediate exposure.

The acquiring organisation should validate the target environment, identify critical assets and dependencies, review privileged access, assess material supplier risks and establish an authoritative view of cybersecurity risks requiring treatment.

## 4. Key Activities

| Domain | Activity | Expected Deliverable / Evidence | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Asset Management | Validate physical, cloud and digital assets | Reconciled asset inventory | Critical |
| Identity & Access | Review privileged and administrative accounts | Privileged access review | Critical |
| Risk Management | Consolidate due-diligence findings into the integration risk register | Updated M&A cyber risk register | Critical |
| Third-Party Risk | Assess critical suppliers and outsourced services | Critical supplier risk assessment | High |
| Vulnerability Management | Validate critical and high-risk vulnerabilities | Prioritised remediation plan | Critical |
| Security Monitoring | Confirm logging and monitoring coverage for critical systems | Monitoring coverage assessment | High |
| Incident Response | Review escalation, notification and response arrangements | Incident response gap assessment | High |
| Business Continuity | Validate critical services, recovery dependencies and existing BCP/DR arrangements | Initial resilience assessment | High |

---

## 5. Days 0–30 Key Performance Indicators

| KPI | Target |
|---|---|
| Critical asset discovery | 100% of identified critical assets recorded |
| Privileged access review | 100% of known privileged accounts reviewed |
| Critical supplier assessment | 100% of identified critical suppliers assessed |
| Risk registration | Material cybersecurity findings recorded and assigned |
| Critical vulnerability tracking | Critical vulnerabilities entered into formal remediation workflow |

### Phase 1 Exit Criteria

Phase 1 should not be considered complete until:

- Critical assets and services are identified
- Material privileged access has been reviewed
- Critical suppliers have been assessed
- High and critical cybersecurity risks have accountable owners
- Immediate containment or remediation actions are established
- Executive stakeholders understand material Day-1 and early integration risks

---

# Phase 2: Days 31–60 — Align, Remediate and Integrate

## 6. Phase Objective

Days 31–60 focus on reducing identified risk and aligning the acquired organisation with the acquiring organisation's security governance and control environment.

The emphasis moves from discovery to remediation, policy harmonisation, identity integration, resilience improvement and workforce security alignment.

## 7. Key Activities

| Domain | Activity | Expected Deliverable / Evidence | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Security Governance | Harmonise priority information security policies | Approved unified policy framework | High |
| Identity & Access | Integrate core IAM capabilities and access governance | IAM integration evidence | Critical |
| Vulnerability Management | Remediate critical and priority vulnerabilities | Remediation and validation evidence | Critical |
| Business Continuity | Review and update BCP/DR arrangements | Updated BCP/DR documentation | High |
| Security Awareness | Deliver required security awareness training | Training completion evidence | Medium |
| Third-Party Risk | Progress remediation for high-risk suppliers | Supplier remediation tracker | High |
| Data Protection | Review priority data flows and integration risks | Updated data-flow/DPIA evidence where applicable | High |
| Incident Response | Align incident escalation and communication processes | Harmonised incident response procedures | High |

---

## 8. Days 31–60 Key Performance Indicators

| KPI | Target |
|---|---|
| Policy harmonisation | Priority security policies reviewed, approved and published |
| IAM integration | Core identity stores integrated or formally transitioned |
| Critical vulnerability remediation | Critical vulnerabilities remediated within agreed SLA |
| High vulnerability remediation | High-risk vulnerabilities tracked against agreed SLA |
| BCP/DR review | Critical-service continuity arrangements reviewed and updated |
| Security awareness | ≥95% overall training completion |
| High-risk users | 100% completion for designated high-risk users |

### Phase 2 Exit Criteria

Phase 2 should demonstrate that:

- Priority policies are aligned
- Critical IAM integration risks are controlled
- Critical vulnerabilities have been remediated or formally risk-accepted
- BCP and DR requirements for critical services are documented
- High-risk supplier remediation is progressing
- Required security awareness has been delivered
- Material integration risks are being actively monitored

---

# Phase 3: Days 61–90 — Assure, Govern and Optimise

## 9. Phase Objective

The final phase focuses on security assurance, governance maturity and transition into sustainable business-as-usual security management.

The organisation should validate the effectiveness of implemented controls, progress ISMS integration, complete appropriate assurance activities and provide executives with a clear view of residual risk and integration status.

## 10. Key Activities

| Domain | Activity | Expected Deliverable / Evidence | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| ISMS | Integrate applicable security controls into the unified ISMS | ISMS integration evidence | Critical |
| Internal Audit | Conduct targeted internal security/compliance audit | Internal audit report | High |
| Security Testing | Conduct penetration testing or appropriate technical assurance | Security testing report | High |
| Risk Management | Reassess residual risks following remediation | Updated residual risk register | Critical |
| Executive Reporting | Produce security integration dashboard | Executive security dashboard | High |
| Management Review | Conduct formal integration management review | Management review record | High |
| Business Resilience | Validate priority recovery arrangements | Recovery/continuity assurance evidence | High |
| Continuous Improvement | Transfer outstanding actions into BAU improvement programme | Continuous improvement plan | Medium |

---

## 11. Days 61–90 Key Performance Indicators

| KPI | Target |
|---|---|
| ISMS integration | Applicable integrated controls mapped to ISMS requirements |
| Internal audit | 100% of agreed audit scope completed |
| Critical audit findings | No unmitigated critical findings at phase closure |
| Penetration testing | Critical findings remediated or formally treated |
| Executive reporting | Security integration dashboard delivered |
| Management review | Formal management review and sign-off completed |
| Residual risk | Material residual risks documented and formally owned |

### Phase 3 Exit Criteria

The 90-day integration programme should transition into business-as-usual governance when:

- Material integration risks have defined treatment decisions
- Applicable controls are incorporated into the ISMS
- Critical assurance findings are addressed or formally accepted
- Residual risks have accountable owners
- Executive reporting is operational
- Outstanding remediation activities have agreed owners and deadlines
- Continuous monitoring and improvement arrangements are established

---

## 12. Governance and Accountability

Post-acquisition security integration requires collaboration across business and technology functions.

Key stakeholders may include:

- Executive Sponsor
- CIO
- CISO
- Information Security
- Enterprise Architecture
- IT Operations
- Legal
- Privacy
- Procurement
- Human Resources
- Business Continuity
- Project Management Office (PMO)

Accountability should be assigned according to the organisation's governance structure, with material risks and integration decisions escalated through appropriate executive forums.

---

## 13. Reporting Cadence

| Governance Activity | Suggested Frequency | Primary Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| M&A Security Integration Review | Weekly | Track integration progress, dependencies and blockers |
| Cyber Risk Review | Weekly | Review high and critical cybersecurity risks |
| Third-Party Risk Review | Bi-weekly | Track critical supplier risks and remediation |
| Integration Status Report | Weekly | Report milestones, actions and delivery status |
| Executive Security Dashboard | Monthly | Provide leadership visibility of risk and performance |
| Management Review | At key integration milestones | Review effectiveness, residual risk and strategic decisions |

---

## 14. Executive Reporting

Executive reporting should provide a concise view of:

- High and critical risks
- Risk treatment progress
- Critical vulnerability remediation
- IAM integration status
- Third-party risk
- Business continuity and resilience
- Security assurance findings
- ISMS integration progress
- Key dependencies and blockers
- Decisions requiring executive approval

Reporting should focus on business impact and decision-making rather than technical activity alone.

---

## 15. 90-Day Outcome

At the end of the initial 90-day period, the acquiring organisation should have moved from **due-diligence visibility** to **controlled security integration**.

The intended progression is:

**Due Diligence → Risk Prioritisation → Stabilisation → Remediation → Control Integration → Assurance → Executive Oversight → Continuous Improvement**

The roadmap does not imply that all cybersecurity integration activities will be completed within 90 days. Longer-term activities should transition into an agreed security improvement programme with defined ownership, priorities, milestones and governance.

## Disclaimer

This document is a sanitised portfolio sample created to demonstrate a structured approach to post-acquisition cybersecurity integration. Timelines, risk criteria, controls, governance arrangements and performance targets should be adapted to the size, complexity, risk appetite, regulatory obligations and technology environment of the organisations involved.
