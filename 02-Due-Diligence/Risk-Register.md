## 1. Purpose

This risk register demonstrates a structured approach to identifying, assessing, treating and monitoring cybersecurity risks identified during merger and acquisition (M&A) due diligence.

The register supports executive decision-making by distinguishing between risks that may affect the transaction and risks that can be managed through post-acquisition remediation and integration.

This is a sanitised portfolio example and does not contain confidential client or employer information.

---

## 2. Risk Assessment Methodology

Each identified risk is assessed using **Likelihood** and **Impact**.

### Likelihood

| Rating             | Description                          |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------ |
| 1 – Rare           | Unlikely to occur                    |
| 2 – Unlikely       | Could occur but is not expected      |
| 3 – Possible       | May occur under normal circumstances |
| 4 – Likely         | Expected to occur                    |
| 5 – Almost Certain | Expected to occur frequently         |

### Impact

| Rating            | Description                                             |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1 – Insignificant | Minimal operational or financial impact                 |
| 2 – Minor         | Limited business disruption                             |
| 3 – Moderate      | Material operational, financial or compliance impact    |
| 4 – Major         | Significant business, regulatory or reputational impact |
| 5 – Severe        | Critical impact on the organisation or transaction      |

### Risk Score

**Risk Score = Likelihood × Impact**

| Score | Rating   |
| ----- | -------- |
| 1–4   | Low      |
| 5–9   | Medium   |
| 10–16 | High     |
| 17–25 | Critical |

## 4. Priority Risks for Transaction Review

The following risks require particular attention during the transaction:

### Third-Party and Supply Chain Risk

Critical suppliers can introduce operational, security and regulatory dependencies that may not be immediately visible during acquisition.

Due diligence should therefore establish:

* supplier criticality;
* access to organisational systems and data;
* assurance evidence;
* contractual security obligations;
* incident notification requirements;
* concentration risk; and
* fourth-party dependencies where material.

### Identity and Privileged Access

Privileged access represents a significant integration risk because excessive, dormant or poorly governed administrative accounts may provide opportunities for unauthorised access.

Priority activities should include:

* privileged account discovery;
* MFA validation;
* access recertification;
* removal of dormant accounts;
* segregation of duties; and
* privileged access monitoring.

### Business Continuity and Resilience

The acquiring organisation should establish whether critical services can continue following a significant cyber or operational disruption.

Evidence should include:

* Business Impact Analysis;
* recovery objectives;
* disaster recovery arrangements;
* backup testing;
* crisis management procedures; and
* evidence of continuity exercises.

## 5. Risk Treatment Approach

Risks identified through due diligence may be managed using four recognised treatment options:

* **Mitigate** – implement controls to reduce likelihood or impact.
* **Avoid** – discontinue or change the activity creating the risk.
* **Transfer** – transfer part of the financial or operational exposure through contractual arrangements or insurance.
* **Accept** – formally accept risk within approved risk appetite and tolerance.

High and critical risks should be escalated to appropriate decision-makers and assigned accountable owners.

## 6. Deal Impact Classification

Cybersecurity findings should be considered in the context of the transaction rather than treated purely as technical issues.

| Classification          | Meaning                                          | Example Response                                |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Deal Breaker            | Risk may make acquisition unacceptable           | Escalate for executive/legal decision           |
| Pre-Close Condition     | Must be addressed before completion              | Remediate or establish contractual protection   |
| Day-1 Priority          | Requires immediate control following acquisition | Include in Day-1 security plan                  |
| 30–90 Day Remediation   | Can be managed during integration                | Add to integration roadmap                      |
| Longer-Term Improvement | Does not materially affect transaction           | Incorporate into security improvement programme |

---

## 7. Governance and Monitoring

Each material risk should have:

* a named risk owner;
* an agreed treatment action;
* a target completion date;
* defined evidence of remediation;
* residual risk assessment; and
* appropriate executive oversight.

Progress should be reported through the post-acquisition governance structure until risks are either remediated or formally accepted.

---

## 8. Executive Conclusion

Cybersecurity risk during an acquisition should be evaluated not only by the number of findings identified but also by their potential effect on **transaction value, regulatory exposure, operational resilience and integration complexity**.

The risk register therefore provides a traceable link between:

**Due Diligence → Risk Identification → Deal Impact → Risk Treatment → Integration → Executive Assurance**

This approach enables cybersecurity to support informed acquisition decisions while providing a structured basis for post-acquisition remediation.
