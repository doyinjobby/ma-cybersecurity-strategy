# M&A Third-Party Assurance Evidence Checklist

**Author:** Dr. Olajumoke Bello, PhD  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Classification:** Public Portfolio Sample  
**Status:** Illustrative M&A Third-Party Assurance Deliverable

---

## 1. Purpose

This checklist provides a structured approach for reviewing cybersecurity assurance evidence supplied by third-party suppliers during merger and acquisition (M&A) due diligence.

The objective is to determine whether submitted evidence is current, relevant, complete and sufficient to support the supplier's security assertions.

Evidence should not be accepted solely because a document has been provided. The reviewer should consider its scope, validity, ownership, applicability and whether it demonstrates that the relevant control is operating effectively.

The depth of evidence review should be proportionate to supplier criticality and risk tier.

---

## 2. Evidence Review Principles

For each item of evidence, consider:

- Is the evidence current?
- Is it relevant to the service being assessed?
- Does its scope cover the systems, locations and services relied upon?
- Has it been independently validated where appropriate?
- Does it demonstrate control design only, or control operation?
- Are exceptions, findings or qualifications identified?
- Are remediation actions documented?
- Is there a named owner?
- Is additional evidence required?
- Does the evidence change the supplier's residual risk?

Evidence should be recorded as:

- **Satisfactory** — evidence adequately supports the control
- **Partially Satisfactory** — evidence exists but gaps or limitations remain
- **Unsatisfactory** — evidence does not adequately support the control
- **Not Provided** — requested evidence has not been supplied
- **Not Applicable** — requirement does not apply to the supplier or service

---

## 3. Information Security Governance

Review evidence such as:

- Information Security Policy
- Security governance structure
- Roles and responsibilities
- Cybersecurity risk-management methodology
- Information-security risk register
- Internal audit programme
- Management review records
- Security objectives and metrics

### Review Considerations

Confirm that governance documentation is approved, current, applicable to the assessed service and supported by evidence of implementation.

---

## 4. ISO 27001 Assurance

Where ISO/IEC 27001 certification is relied upon, review:

- Certificate validity
- Certification body
- Certification dates
- Scope of certification
- Relevant locations
- Relevant services and technology
- Statement of Applicability where available and appropriate
- Surveillance or recertification status
- Material audit findings where disclosed

### Review Considerations

An ISO 27001 certificate should not automatically be treated as sufficient assurance.

Confirm that the certification scope actually covers the service, environment or organisational entity being assessed.

---

## 5. SOC 2 Assurance

Where a SOC 2 report is available, review:

- Report type
- Reporting period
- Trust Services Criteria covered
- Service scope
- Auditor opinion
- Control exceptions
- Complementary User Entity Controls
- Subservice organisations
- Management responses
- Remediation actions

### Review Considerations

For ongoing assurance, a SOC 2 Type II report generally provides stronger evidence of control operation over a defined period than a point-in-time assessment.

Material exceptions should be evaluated for their relevance to the service and associated M&A risk.

---

## 6. Identity and Access Management

Review evidence such as:

- Access-control policy
- Joiner-Mover-Leaver procedure
- MFA implementation evidence
- Privileged Access Management controls
- Access recertification records
- Privileged account inventory
- Authentication standards
- Third-party access controls

### Review Considerations

Confirm that privileged and remote access is appropriately controlled, periodically reviewed and removed when no longer required.

---

## 7. Vulnerability Management and Security Testing

Review evidence such as:

- Vulnerability-management policy
- Vulnerability scan summaries
- Patch-management process
- Remediation SLAs
- Penetration-test executive summaries
- Outstanding vulnerability reports
- Remediation trackers
- Security-testing schedules

### Review Considerations

Determine whether critical and high-risk vulnerabilities are identified, prioritised, remediated and tracked within defined timescales.

Where significant findings remain open, assess whether compensating controls or formal risk acceptance exist.

---

## 8. Data Protection and Privacy

Review evidence such as:

- Data Processing Agreement
- Privacy policies
- Data-flow documentation
- DPIAs where applicable
- Records of Processing Activities where relevant
- Retention and deletion requirements
- International transfer arrangements
- Subprocessor register
- Data-breach procedures

### Review Considerations

Confirm that the supplier understands what personal information it processes, where the information is located, applicable retention requirements and relevant subprocessor dependencies.

---

## 9. Cloud and Infrastructure Security

Review evidence such as:

- Cloud architecture diagrams
- Security configuration standards
- Encryption standards
- Cloud security assessments
- Logging and monitoring configuration
- Network architecture
- Environment segregation controls
- Backup arrangements
- Shared-responsibility documentation

### Review Considerations

Determine whether security responsibilities between the supplier, customer and cloud provider are clearly understood and appropriately implemented.

---

## 10. Security Monitoring and Incident Response

Review evidence such as:

- Incident Response Plan
- Incident severity model
- Escalation procedures
- Security monitoring documentation
- Incident register
- Tabletop exercise results
- Lessons-learned reports
- Customer notification procedures

### Review Considerations

Assess whether the supplier can detect, respond to, escalate and communicate cybersecurity incidents within appropriate timescales.

Material historical incidents should be considered alongside evidence of corrective action.

---

## 11. Business Continuity and Disaster Recovery

Review evidence such as:

- Business Continuity Plan
- Business Impact Analysis
- Disaster Recovery Plan
- Recovery Time Objectives
- Recovery Point Objectives
- Backup procedures
- Recovery-test results
- Continuity exercise reports
- Corrective-action records

### Review Considerations

Confirm that continuity and recovery arrangements have been tested rather than relying solely on documented plans.

Determine whether recovery objectives align with the business dependency on the supplier.

---

## 12. Fourth-Party and Supply Chain Assurance

Review evidence such as:

- Subcontractor inventory
- Subprocessor list
- Fourth-party risk assessments
- Supplier assurance procedures
- Contractual flow-down requirements
- Cloud-provider dependencies
- Concentration-risk assessments
- Fourth-party incident arrangements

### Review Considerations

Identify dependencies that could create material exposure even where the direct supplier appears adequately controlled.

---

## 13. Contractual Security Evidence

Review contractual documentation for:

- Information-security obligations
- Data-protection requirements
- Incident-notification requirements
- Audit and assurance rights
- Subcontractor controls
- Business-continuity requirements
- Security-testing obligations where appropriate
- Data return and deletion
- Exit and termination requirements
- Material-change notification

### Review Considerations

Where technical controls appear adequate but contractual protections are insufficient, record the contractual gap as a risk requiring remediation.

---

## 14. Evidence Gaps and Exceptions

Evidence gaps should be documented and assessed rather than treated automatically as control failure.

For each material gap, record:

- Evidence requested
- Evidence received
- Review outcome
- Identified gap
- Risk implication
- Additional evidence required
- Supplier response
- Remediation action
- Risk owner
- Target date
- Residual risk
- Escalation requirement

Material evidence gaps should be incorporated into the M&A Cybersecurity Risk Register where they create transaction or integration risk.

---

## 15. M&A Escalation

Evidence findings may result in classification as:

- **Deal Breaker**
- **Pre-Close Condition**
- **Day-1 Priority**
- **30–90 Day Remediation**
- **Longer-Term Improvement**

The classification should reflect supplier criticality, control weakness, business dependency, regulatory exposure and remediation complexity.

---

## 16. Assurance Outcome

At completion of the evidence review, the assessor should be able to determine:

- Whether sufficient evidence has been provided
- Whether key controls appear appropriately designed and operating
- Whether material control gaps exist
- Whether additional evidence is required
- Whether remediation is required
- Whether residual risk is acceptable
- Whether the issue requires escalation
- Whether post-acquisition monitoring is necessary

The evidence-assurance lifecycle is:

**Evidence Request → Evidence Collection → Validation → Gap Identification → Risk Assessment → Remediation → Residual Risk → Ongoing Assurance**

---

## Disclaimer

This checklist is a sanitised portfolio example created for professional and educational demonstration. It does not contain confidential client or employer information. Evidence requirements and assurance activities should be tailored to supplier criticality, applicable regulation, contractual obligations, organisational risk appetite and the nature of the service being assessed.
